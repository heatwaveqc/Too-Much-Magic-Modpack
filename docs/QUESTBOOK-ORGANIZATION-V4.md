# TMM Questbook Organization v4.1 — five structural categories, mod capstones and completionism

**Owner design directions — 2026-11-22.** Architecture and authoring requirements for Too Much Magic (TMM) Minecraft 1.21.1 NeoForge. **Design-only:** proposed chapter groups and new tasks are **not** present in the shipped FTB questbook merely because this document exists. The currently installed scaffold uses five groups and 44 chapters. Preserve its stable chapter, quest, task, reward and per-player claim IDs when rearranging; migrate chapter group/position without deleting historical progress. This proposal supersedes the *organizational choices* in [QUEST-ARCHITECTURE.md](QUEST-ARCHITECTURE.md), not the current manifest, and complements [QUEST-DESIGN-RULES.md](QUEST-DESIGN-RULES.md), [QUEST-WALKTHROUGH-STANDARD.md](QUEST-WALKTHROUGH-STANDARD.md), and the [v3 visual standard](QUEST-PRESENTATION-STANDARD-V3.md) (PR #489).

## 1. Design principles — accepted

1. **Every actual installed mod is discoverable.** Every gameplay/content mod must be named in at least one quest chapter, meaningful topic section or discoverable mod index. Substantive independent systems with progression deserve a coherent walkthrough chapter; addons have distinct branches in their parent chapters. Configuration, performance and library mods should appear in an explicit **Installed Mods & Technical Credits** index and may receive functional Tips & Tricks entries when player-facing. Do not invent crafting quests for API libraries or require unavailable/inactive candidates. A chapter-title inventory by itself does not prove coverage.
2. **Teaching, practice, challenges, jobs are different.** Teach the full mod with a walkthrough; give practice objectives in the relevant topical chapter; make permanent personal feats and breadth-completion in Challenges; leave renewable contract generation, timers, delivery, reputation and payouts to native Bountiful. A player who simply wants to start using a mod should not first need to kill 100 monsters or eat every food.
3. **Cross-mod compatibility is documented bidirectionally.** Every explicit native or TMM-authored compatibility relationship gets an entry in each relevant mod's chapter or section, with a local explanation of what actually works, how to use it, where the counterpart explains it, and any conditional requirements. Neither a mere shared dependency nor compatibility wishlist is proof of functional integration.
4. **Complete challenges represent actual actions, not inventory proxies.** Catch means generated by a real eligible fishing action, not owning five fish obtained from a chest, shop, EMC or crafting. Eat means successful player consumption, not possession. Learn means acquisition of knowledge in the mod's real per-player state. Kill means credited eligible kill event, not holding a mob drop or a spawn egg. A task unable to detect the action must be labelled manual/blocked, not silently substituted.
5. **Stable and achievable catalogs.** Generate fish, foods, spells/glyphs, mob types and game-content eligibility from the exact pinned/loaded mod set plus documented owner inclusion/exclusion policy. Exclude unregistered, technical/test-only, unobtainable or no-real-player-route entries from **obligatory** completion sets, with evidence. Keep a reviewable reason list for exclusions; do not silently omit hard-but-attainable content. Freeze a catalog version for stable saved progress, and migrate denominators/identities explicitly on catalog updates.
6. **Paths/form access and diets are respected.** Challenges must not accidentally require simultaneous mutually exclusive player forms. The all-food challenge needs special review when the shared food policy/diets are reopened; until then it is a design target, not a code-ready task. Transformations remain actual mechanics enforced in Progression/native mods; an FTB quest does not grant a form, bypass a step or create an alternative mode.

## 2. Five category *types* and their distinct quest grammar

**Owner correction 2026-11-22:** Replace the proposed seven top-level topic groups with **five top-level categories by quest purpose**. This is a structural authoring rule, not simply a rebranding of the old Magic/Endgame grouping. Keep real mod chapters intact, preserve all 44 existing chapter IDs, and revise source group assignments only through a separately reviewed FTB migration. The category order below is intentional; the Challenge category is last.

| Category | Example chapters | Required shape and authoring contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Informational** | Welcome to TMM; Getting Started; Tips & Tricks | Browse-first short reference cards, navigation links and verified mechanics; no invented possession chores or unnecessary linear prerequisites. The personal Newbie→Normie graduation task is a legitimate explicit exception, with authoritative Progression state rather than a mere instructional checkmark. |
| **Regular Progress** | Basic Progression; Advanced Progression; Paths & Transformations; Endgame Roadmap; A Sufficiently Advanced Magic | **One pack-wide advancement spine** from survival into endgame with sensible choice points and pointers to mod-focused chapters. Don't copy full specialist lines or rewards. The Paths chapter shows how to acquire each currently implemented Path/form, their limitations and exceptional combinations. |
| **Mod-Focused** | Ars Nouveau; Theurgy; Occultism; Botania; Cataclysm; Draconic Evolution; ProjectE; Replication | **One central mod mainline** containing its essential tiers, techniques, structures and rituals. End on the **hardest or most defining meaningful craft/ritual/achievement the mod treats as its capstone**, not merely the highest-cost recipe. Attach separate optional branches for **useful items**, **addons** and **explicit compatibility with other mods**, at their actual introduction points. The mainline's finale should mention and link to any meaningful TMM crossover/endgame integration. |
| **Thematic** | Food; Fishing; Monster Hunting (Mob Hunts); Alcohol is Magic | Wide, frequently non-linear multi-mod activity families. Food has clear branches for each relevant food mod, Fishing for fishing systems/species families, and Mob Hunts for credited kill-count ladders. Local milestones can be substantial, but **all-of-a-kind completionist goals belong in Challenge**, not at the end of these ordinary topical lines. |
| **Challenge** | Bounties & Contracts; Master Angler; Master Chef; Master of Glyphs; Bestiary | Entirely separate, opt-in late-book category with **two subtypes**: one-time, persistent completionist **Challenges**, and **Bounties** (renewable Bountiful contracts with native timers and rewards). Teach Bountiful mechanics here; Introduction may include a simple link, not a second bounty chapter. No ordinary progression prerequisite can depend on completing either subtype. |

### Mod-Focused spine and branches — the default chapter template

```text
MOD INTRO → FOUNDATION → CORE TECHNIQUES / TIERS → HIGHER RITES → CAPSTONE
                   ├── Useful items and optional craftable tools
                   ├── Addons and native expansions
                   └── Explicit integrations / reciprocal links to other mod chapters
```

The branches start where the player can actually use them; don't hide a helpful early item behind late-game tasks or require an addon for vanilla mod progress. Branches may have their own advanced mini-goals. If a cross-mod integration has a genuine required dependency in TMM, represent that dependency honestly; **optional** compat routes do not become a mainline gate merely because they are interesting. The final mainline should be the **meaningful hardest ritual/craft**, even if cheaper than another item. It includes a short "Where to go next" entry or reciprocal links to TMM-specific integrations rather than making every cross-mod experiment mandatory.

**Occultism example (owner proposed):** Native Occultism ritual tiers, spirit summoning, and high-tier **Eldritch Ancient Miner** lead to a **TMM-specific Philosopher's Stone crossover finale**. The Stone is a **ProjectE item**, not an Occultism-native craft; treating it as Occultism's final mainline quest is an **intentional TMM extension/exception** to the native-capstone rule. It should point into ProjectE and any boss-token/recipe dependencies; preserve their actual independent server-side gates. Place dimensional storage/remote access, spirit services, material processing, miner/Replication interactions and Theurgy logistics in meaningful **useful items / integration side branches**, with a reciprocal entry in their respective mod chapters. Avoid duplicating first-time rewards/claims across ProjectE and Occultism for the same craft.

**Five types are not five exclusivity rules for mechanics.** A thematic Food quest can link to a mod-focused Farmer's Delight lane if depth warrants one; an introductory page can point to Challenge bounties; and a final craft can legitimately be a crossover. The categories define **the reader's expectation and layout**, not mutually exclusive mod lists.

> **OWNER-LOCKED 2026-11-22 — chapter visual grammar.** A chapter's graph must make its reading order, main route, optional branches, and endpoint apparent before opening quest descriptions. Treat this as an acceptance requirement, not merely an art suggestion. The five category archetypes below govern authoring; mod-focused chapters retain a defining hard craft/ritual capstone, and Theurgy is the first retrofit case. Type-A review checks that dependency edges retain their *effective* prerequisites, remain readable with minimal line crossings, keep art outside nodes/links, and preserve stable IDs; Type-C in-client readability still needs a player-visible check at normal/reduced zoom.

### Approved TMM visual grammar — reuse v3 authoring archetypes

Apply the existing [Quest Presentation and Visual Authoring Standard v3](QUEST-PRESENTATION-STANDARD-V3.md), rather than inventing another conflicting art or node-shape standard:

1. **Informational → open cards / loose hub.** A prominent orientation node and short independent tips. No invented completion chain. Use descriptive icons and compact visual grouping.
2. **Regular Progress → forward spine with real forks.** Prefer left-to-right for TMM consistency. Each major gate/choice expands into nearby off-axis side routes; the reader sees the next core milestone without tracing a maze.
3. **Mod-Focused → essential native progression spine + labeled side clusters.** Introduction/guide → tiered setup → significant rituals/machines → **hardest defining capstone**. Useful items, optional equipment, native addons, and verified **reciprocal** mod compatibility each have identifiable branches at the right tier. Special cases, such as Occultism → ProjectE Philosopher's Stone, explicitly mark the crossover as a TMM-defined capstone. A branching or multi-root native mod is not squeezed into a fictional single dependency.
4. **Thematic → grouped activity lanes and compact task families.** Food by food mod/craft activity, Fishing by method/species, Monster Hunting by creature family and count ladder. Small grids make repetitive objectives scannable. Whole-catalog mastery is **Challenge**, never a thematic mandatory capstone.
5. **Challenge → separate completionist displays and bounty board.** Permanent all-of-a-kind achievements use visible independent grids/convergent completion. Bountiful jobs remain renewable in its own native board; the FTB quest chapter should only teach and link to that system, not manufacture pseudo-repeatable FTB kill quests.

**Visual consistency rules across all five types:** prominent recognizable entry; normal-sized recurring actions; distinct section/branch labels; major native tiers and final capstones made prominent by verified shape, relative size, icon and *original* chapter art; align repeated nodes; leave negative space between lanes; minimize dependency crossings; preserve real item/task semantics and existing quest IDs. The v3 standard already covers original header/panel art and source-backed icon validation. **No universal numeric spacing or shape-to-role key is locked until the pinned FTB client confirms readability.**

## 3. Food: its own multi-mod chapter

The Food chapter is the **multi-lane, center-out** hub for growing, collecting, preparing, cooking and eating food. Its central onboarding teaches the actual controls and relevant JEI categories. Give each installed food system a *recognizable side branch*, such as:

- **Farmer's Delight**: cutting board, tools, cooking pot, multi-step dishes and related installed addons with genuine recipes.
- **Pam's HarvestCraft 2**: Crops, Trees, Food Core and Food Extended each as clearly labeled sub-lanes under one Pam's branch rather than four duplicate chapters.
- **Cooking for Blockheads**: modular kitchen, cooking table/oven, recipe discovery and integration with available ingredients.
- **Farming for Blockheads**: market acquisition and seed/sapling sourcing.
- **Other installed farming/food mods** (including Harvest with Ease, Aquaculture Delight or [Let's Do] Farm & Charm *only if actually pinned in this build*): meaningful shortcuts/specific production branches; absence from the packed roster means a research/watch item rather than a fictional quest.
- **Alcohol is Magic** retains a *separate* 19-quest introductory-style chapter linked from Food because brewing, temperature, fermentation and cocktails have their own substantive progression. Do not duplicate the same crafted-item criteria in two chapters.

**Migration:** current `food_fishing.snbt` has 22 nodes, including farm, fish and kitchen objectives. Transfer their existing IDs to Food or Fishing by stable ID; any quest moved between chapters must be checked against FTB's save/claim persistence and localized key rules. Existing rewards, owner rules and players' completion are not disposable. No mass-rewrite of the food/diet engine while its owner hold remains.

## 4. Fishing: a large discovery and mastery chapter

Fishing is a separate sprawling, branching chapter, organized by **native fishing system, location/biome/water, equipment, and species family**, with a visible catalog index and themed aquatic artwork. Start with vanilla fishing, then progress through Aquaculture rods and meaningful installed fishing mechanics. Add one dedicated quest for **catch five of each eligible fish species** in the *actual selected pack*. For a truly collectible species define its **native item/entity identity** and what the fishing event actually yields; avoid counting cookery outputs, generic salvage or decorative non-fish catches as fish.

**Catch detection:** a five-fish item task proves only inventory possession, not five fish legitimately caught. The preferred implementation is a verified native catch event or a minimal shared, per-player catch receipt/counter (TMM-owned code, not a new independent progression engine). It must identify the actual loot/result, item counts, rod/fishing system and attribution; ignore crafted, traded, EMC-duplicated, looted, gifted or admin-spawned fish. Account for multiple output stacks, fishing loot modifiers, non-vanilla fishing methods, multiplayer and inventory overflow. Don't invent a working FTB task type before an exact current API audit. If catch events can't be evidenced, classify those tasks as **detection-blocked** rather than quietly using possession.

**Fishing completionist challenge:** `Catch Every Fish` should live in **Challenge → Master Angler** and be informed by independently completed Fishing species tasks; it is never a required gate to move into Magic, Food or later rods. **Proposed distinctive prize:** **one Apotheosis Godforged Pearl**, candidate item `apotheosis:godforged_pearl`. Exact item ID and availability must be verified against TMM's installed 1.21.1 registry and economy/reward shortcut policy. This is a candidate reward, not permission to grant a late-game rarity item before the current balance review; the owner can retain this reward after the audit.

## 5. Mob Hunts (persistent FTB feats), not Bountiful

Use a separate **Mob Hunts** chapter under **Thematic**, titled **Monster Hunting** in navigation if desired. The user-supplied ATM10 `bounty_board.snbt` is a **layout/mechanics reference only**: it contains **50 quests, 87 native FTB `type: "kill"` tasks over 63 named entity IDs** and escalating **5 → 10 → 50 → 100** kill ladders for ordinary mobs, plus boss encounters. Reuse neither ATM's own IDs, text, images, reward tables nor unsafe selection of NPC targets.

- Per eligible common hostile mob, use a legible short independent tier ladder. Provisional challenge counts **5, 10, 50, 100** from the reference; tune per mob rarity, invulnerability, spawn category and player enjoyment. Rare bosses normally deserve a **first eligible kill/defeat** quest, not forced repeat 100 kills.
- Native `type: "kill"` task with `entity` and `value` is demonstrated by the ATM10 archive, but must be confirmed on TMM's pinned FTB 2101.x before promotion. Don't confuse Hunter Path XP with FTB kill count: both may receive the same real event without double-awarding a quest.
- Eligible mob population comes from the actual loaded entity registry + the existing TMM Creature Taxonomy/Path Hunter evidence; deliberate exclusions include decorative/technical entities, summons with no legitimate kill route, and protected NPCs or players. Preserve legitimate hostile modded species, bosses and variants when valid.
- Keep mobs visible by family or dimension (Undead, Illagers, Nether, End/Void, beasts, etc.) and distinguish native boss progression from optional personal combat-volume feats.
- Each ladder is **permanent, one-time rewardable** (not repeatable job tickets). A player may progress independently of Bountiful job selection. Native FTB personal/team credit semantics, source attribution, farmed mob handling and claimant permissions require explicit QA.

**Bounties & Contracts** belongs under **Challenge**, alongside but mechanically distinct from permanent completionist achievements: find/craft the board, learn Decrees, accept native Bountiful jobs, deliver, redeem and understand expirations. Introduction merely links players to the bounty area. All repeatable tasks, board job lotteries, payouts, refresh and reputation remain in Bountiful's native runtime, currently with **three** live basic decree families: Hunting, Fishing, Culinary. It is distinct from Mob Hunts and from the **Hunter** specialty Path.

## 6. Paths & Transformations: one authoritative how-to chapter

Rename/evolve the existing `Skills & Factions` source chapter into **Paths & Transformations** (retain persistent chapter/overview IDs). Structure it as an orientation hub with an **honest initiation/how-to branch for each actually available Path/form**, plus practical incompatibility and combination diagrams. Include the two baseline Paths **Newbie → personal graduation → Normie**, universal shared Core progression and MXP (not themselves separate specialty Paths), and all current or admitted specialist routes: Monk, Hunter, Gilded if implemented, Vampire (JAWR), Werewolf (JAWR), Dragon Survival species (Cave/Forest/Sea), the two distinct native Lich routes, and allowed exceptional combinations such as Archlich or Dracolich *only after validated in the actual build*. Additional owner-discussed Sapient Slime, Dryad, Incubus/Succubus, Spawn, Bare Zerker etc. are candidates, **not** ready-made quests until their initiation and runtime are approved/implemented.

Each branch must explicitly state: prerequisites, exact initiation action, whether that action is personal/permanent/reversible, actual Path/form compatibility, diets if settled, skills/power source, level progress, special resource requirements, death/respawn behavior, and exit/transition if supported. **No quest grants or withdraws a form by manual checkmark alone.** If a referenced Path is not in the frozen runtime, offer an informational note instead of awarding or requiring nonexistent powers.

Mirror cross-mod initiation in both places: e.g. JAWR chapter describes the **native ritual/witchcraft** and Paths & Transformations describes the player's **actual form transition**. Goety and Forbidden Magic teach their respective Lich crafting/ritual mechanics; the central chapter owns the character choice, combined forms and mutual exclusion. Subsequent core skills remain global and not tied to Path as required by Progression.

## 7. Challenge category last: completionist feats and native bounties

**Last top-level category**, entirely independent of core progression. Its **Challenges** subsection contains persistent, optional one-time feats designed for many hours; its **Bounties** subsection introduces and links to renewable native Bountiful jobs and Decrees. These two systems must not share the same quest completion semantics. Proposed core one-time achievements:

| Challenge | Correct evidence | Separate source chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Master Angler** — catch every eligible fish (five each in Fishing) | Server-authoritative fish catch receipts, per species and catalog version | Fishing |
| **Master Chef** — successfully eat every eligible food | Actual successful consumption per item/variant, respecting the unsettled shared diet engine and mutually exclusive forms | Food |
| **Master of Glyphs** — unlock every eligible glyph | Ars Nouveau's actual per-player learned-glyph state; Hex Casting patterns/knowledge require a *separate* semantically valid catalog if added | Ars Nouveau / relevant magic addons |
| **Bestiary** — personally defeat every eligible mob species | Credited native FTB kill tasks and/or audited personal kill receipts, catalog distinct-ID reconciliation | Mob Hunts plus boss/dimension chapters |
| **Future complete-a-system feats** | Specific real event/state, never possession guessed to imply it | Corresponding system chapter |

**Catalog ownership:** every catalog has an explicit snapshot version, source mod pins, include/exclude IDs and why, eligibility detector, required per-player action, and a persisted receipt set or safe FTB task mapping. Future new mod pins require a visible version/migration decision, avoiding a surprise moving goalpost after a player legitimately completed an achievement. Rewards are one-time, personal and meaningful, never a boss gate bypass or automatic completion of unrelated item tasks. Complex capstones must never be the only way to obtain mandatory progression resources.

**All-food blocker:** the owner is changing the food handling engine and dragon/form diet rules. Do not activate an impossible food catalog while that policy is on hold. Display the intended challenge as under development, not an apparently achievable 0%-forever progress bar.

## 8. Symmetric cross-mod integration registry — required from authoring

Maintain one **compatibility edge record** per real bridge, not two untracked prose copies:

- **Bridge ID**, first mod ID/chapter + second mod ID/chapter
- **Type:** native official addon / native automatic integration / TMM-authored KubeJS, data/tag or first-party adapter / observed unverified compatibility / speculative candidate. Require verified evidence and pinpoint the correct installed versions.
- **What the player can actually do**, concrete input/output, setup, specific guidebook/JEI location and relevant limitations (resources, sides, fluid vs item, power, protection tags, stages).
- **Both quest placements** and the optional progression line on each side, with reciprocal chapter links. If one mod has no dedicated chapter, its named Tip or section must contain the inverse link.
- **QA and provenance:** upstream or TMM implementation file + test result; distinguish exact source contract from demonstrated TMM runtime behavior; close on pin updates.

**Immediate first edge candidate:** **Theurgy item connectors/Mercurial logistics ↔ Occultism dimensional storage**.
