## Executive gameplay map

**What the player actually does:** Ars has two related but distinct economies. The player builds custom spells by combining a cast form, effects and augments into a spell stored in a spellbook or compatible casting item; casting spends the player's mana. Separately, Sourcelinks create **Source** from supported events or inputs and Source Jars store it for nearby devices and rituals. Source powers supported processing, ritual effects and magical automation; Scribe glyph selection consumes XP points and ingredients through the separately audited learning workflow. The mod's summoned and befriended creatures extend that automation: Starbuncles move filtered items, Whirlisprigs consume Source to generate plant-associated drops from a diverse grove, Wixies automate supported potion/item recipes, and Drygmys turn nearby living entities into repeatable item and experience-gem output. Exploration supplies Wilden parts, magical plants, creatures, and other progression materials. This is both a flexible combat/utility spell system and a small creature-run base-automation system, not only a spellbook.

**Why it matters in TMM:** Its distinctive contribution is player-authored spell composition plus Source-powered magical processing and creature automation. That complements Iron's authored spellbook/school progression, Occultism's spirit labor and storage, and Neo Vitae's altar economy. These are possible comparison points, not evidence of a working integration. Ars has **seven** separately pinned addon records; addon-specific mechanics are not established by this base profile. The [49-quest pilot PR #74](https://github.com/heatwaveqc/Too-Much-Magic-Modpack/pull/74) covers all seven addons on a separate feature branch; it inspected exact pinned JARs, but is not evidence that those quests have been integrated or client-accepted.

**Quick start:** In JEI, craft the Worn Notebook from a book plus any item in `c:gems/lapis`, then use its *Getting Started* entries as the route map. The Novice Spell Book recipe takes a book and one each of an iron shovel, pickaxe, axe, and sword. Select it on the hotbar and use the configured **Open Spell Book** control to open spellcrafting; assign it in Minecraft Options if unbound. Use JEI to craft a Source Jar and a Sourcelink; an Agronomic link is the natural-growth option and bone meal alone does not trigger crop-growth Source. These recipes are from version-aligned generated data; the actual pack's JEI and recipe overrides still need client confirmation.

### Spellbook tiers, glyph learning and practical progression

The native spellbook route is a **capacity gate for glyph tier**, not a linear research tree. The version-aligned Notebook states that Novice books permit Tier 1 glyphs, Apprentice books permit Tier 2, and Archmage books permit Tier 3; an upgrade carries learned spells forward. Craft the exact upgrade recipes through JEI: Novice → Apprentice uses one obsidian-tag item, 3 diamonds, 2 quartz blocks and 2 blaze-rod-tag items; Apprentice → Archmage uses 3 ender pearls, 2 emeralds, a Totem of Undying, Nether Star and Wilden Tribute. These are vanilla/tag ingredients in generated 5.13.5 data; assembled-pack tag resolution is not client-confirmed.

The **Scribes Table is a recipe-driven glyph press**, not a simple block interaction. Select a glyph recipe, which charges its displayed experience-point cost at selection; valid ingredient item entities in a 5×5×5 area are gathered one at a time. With auto-pull enabled (default), it also searches inventories in its larger surrounding area every second. After all inputs are supplied, crafting runs for 120 ticks and drops the glyph item. If cancelled, consumed ingredients are returned and the charged experience is refunded as an XP orb. In the generated recipe set, 82 core glyph recipes cost 27, 55 or 160 XP; counts are 39, 28 and 15 respectively. Use JEI/the Scribes interface for a player's chosen spell rather than making every optional glyph a mandatory quest.

The output glyph item must then be **used once to learn it**; crafting alone is not learning. The player receives a learned-glyph capability update and the glyph item is consumed. The spellbook GUI enforces its tier against each glyph's configured tier. Thus a useful walkthrough teaches this loop explicitly: select a valid recipe → pay XP → supply ingredients → collect the finished glyph → use it to learn → assemble a valid spell within the held book's tier → cast and observe the mana/effect. FTB's item acquisition task can prove the book/glyph exists, but does not prove that the glyph was learned or that the spell cast.

This makes the basic progression choice-driven after the tier upgrades: the player selects effects/forms/augments that fit their goals, and the Notebook plus JEI expose recipes and descriptions. The profile does not treat all 82 source recipes as required advancement milestones. The pack's source-derived default glyph list and all spell validity/mana combinations still need a dedicated in-client walkthrough.

## Acquisition, renewability and output economics

- Core Source is renewable when its chosen trigger/input is renewable. Agronomic and Vitalic links are event-driven; Alchemical, Mycelial and Volcanic links convert consumed potion/food/fuel inputs.
- Source is stored/handled as Ars capability energy in Source Jars and machines. It is not interchangeable with FE, mana, blood or a fluid based on naming.
- Drygmy output is a conversion of eligible nearby mob drop tables/experience value into item output and experience gems; it spends Source and depends on the mob's happiness and experience. This has high potential to bypass gated drops if eligible boss or rare mobs are included; audit exact entity validity before proposing a pack farm.
- Apparatus/Imbuement outputs are recipe-defined. No universal duplicator claim is supported.
- Ars adds unique Wilden drops and a repeatable summoning route. The Wilden Chimera is a distinct boss and its summoning ritual can damage terrain. It is an obvious progression gate candidate, but any TMM recipe/lock remains unimplemented unless separately confirmed.

## Low-hanging setups useful in ordinary survival

### 1. Crop-adjacent Source starter — early, passive
- **Goal and payoff:** Create a steady trickle of Source while ordinary crops or trees grow.
- **Minimum entry:** Agronomic Sourcelink, one Source Jar, a small farm/tree plot. Exact crafting burden is not stated here.
- **Build/use:** Place Sourcelink beside the farm; place Source Jar next to it; use Source-consuming Ars stations close to the Jar. Prefer several growing crops rather than relying on an unverified modded crop tag.
- **Sustainability and limits:** No crop is consumed by the link; it only reacts to successful growth. Output depends on growth events and correct source/tick conditions.
- **Evidence / pack test:** Upstream source confirms 20 Source per normal crop growth and 50 per normal successful tree growth; not tested in TMM.

### 2. Mycelial food recycler — early, small item sink
- **Goal and payoff:** Convert surplus food into Source; the link also uses stored progress to make nearby mushrooms/mycelium.
- **Minimum entry:** Mycelial Sourcelink, Source Jar, surplus edible item; optional Arcane Pedestal for controlled feed.
- **Build/use:** Feed one item at a time into the link or its surrounding pedestal; connect Jar storage and monitor total Source. Use a safe collection area if allowing mushroom/mycelium growth.
- **Sustainability and limits:** Food is consumed, with crafting remainder preserved; this does not create free food. Better food and magic-food tags raise Source; exact best value should be measured, and modded food tag behavior is unconfirmed.
- **Evidence / pack test:** Conversion formula and consumption path inspected in upstream code; no rate test.

### 3. Drygmy drop-harvesting habitat — midgame, renewable drops
- **Goal and payoff:** Convert eligible nearby entity loot-table outputs and a fraction of their XP into items and experience gems without hurting or consuming the entities.
- **Minimum entry:** Find a wild Drygmy, tame it with a Wilden Horn, Spike or Wing, make a Drygmy Charm, then use the Charm on Mossy Cobblestone to create the Henge. Add Source, an adjacent chest or other item handler, and eligible nearby entities or populated Mob Jars. The charm recipe uses one Drygmy Shard, fish, wheat, apple, carrot, seeds and three Source Gems; check JEI for actual tag matches.
- **Build/use:** Keep the Henge in a safe habitat. Its natural-entity scan is a roughly 10-block horizontal / 6-block vertical region; a Wand can toggle natural world entities, while eligible Mob Jar contents remain included. Each additional Charm can add a worker. Supply at least the default 1,000 Source per generation and enough adjacent inventory capacity; the Henge selects entities itself and shares one progress counter among workers.
- **Sustainability and limits:** Default output target is `1 + 2 × distinct entity types + min(5, nearby entity count)` item counts, after 20 worker-progress units. Candidate loot is generated with an Ars fake-player context; entities are not killed. Experience is summed, quartered, and converted to gems. Blacklist filtering happens late, so a blacklisted mob can still contribute to population/unique-type bonuses. Default blacklist contains only Iron Golems. Boss/rare eligibility is not pack-approved. Full or absent adjacent storage can silently lose item and gem remainders.
- **Evidence / pack test:** Version-aligned 5.13.5 source maps taming, charm, habitat scan, worker, Source and output logic. Exact pinned-JAR equivalence, pack blacklist/tags and client behavior remain unverified. No TMM test performed.

Travel/pathing adds time. Source is paid ahead for the next generation and is not atomically coupled to successful output; no eligible loot or full storage does not guarantee a refund. |
| Generate output | When not needing mana and at/above max progress, the Henge acts on a 100-tick output loop if entities remain. The default target is base 1 + 2 per distinct entity type + up to 5 for nearby entity count. Blacklist filtering occurs after the count/unique-type bonus and candidate setup. | A blacklisted entity can still count toward bonuses and be targeted; the default blacklist contains only `minecraft:iron_golem`. | Resolve drops and XP | For each eligible candidate, the Henge queries its loot table with an Ars fake player assigned as damage source, attacker and last-damage player, including fake-player luck. It does not damage, kill or discard the entity. Missing loot-table keys are skipped. Loot stacks are combined and randomly sampled until the target count is met or exceeded, so stack counts may overshoot. Summed XP is quartered; values over 3 become Greater/Lesser Experience Gems with 12/3 thresholds and a rounded-up remainder. | “Renewable drops” are entity loot-table simulation. | Insert outputs | Adjacent item handlers receive item stacks and gems. The insertion method returns leftovers, but the Henge caller ignores them; no world-drop fallback appears in that path. | Provide adequate adjacent inventory capacity and verify blocked/full-handler behavior before relying on production. |

**Pack policy and test boundary:** Before teaching or using Drygmy production for a valuable target, enumerate target entity IDs and loot tables, inspect the effective blacklist, and compare outputs against boss/rare/item progression gates. Disposable client checks should cover one common passive, one neutral, one boss/rare, one jar-only mob, the natural-entity Wand toggle, boundary distances, multiple workers, insufficient Source, unloaded/reloaded chunks, and full/absent storage. These are proposed checks, not performed results.

### Method improvements surfaced by this review

1. Maintain one canonical **step-level walkthrough table** and link it to each quest; keep separate from a high-level progression overview so missing required actions are visible.
2. Record guidebook **acquisition and opening as mechanics**, with exact recipe and earliest stage; then map named guide pages to every construction-heavy quest.
3. Put **what the player does, what success looks like, and what FTB detects** side by side. Distinguish actual operation, product proxy, item ownership, and manual observation.
4. Keep upstream source version, pack pin/hash, binary inspection, server registry smoke, and client walkthrough as separate evidence levels. A version-aligned commit or server boot is not proof of client progression.
5. Audit walkthrough coverage by required stage and each installed addon. An existing quest pilot is useful evidence and can be reused, but does not make unresearched steps or unaccepted playtests complete.
6. For progression systems with player-selected branches, document the **choice boundary**: mandatory tier/capability gates versus optional recipes and builds. Teach how a player discovers and completes one valid branch through JEI/guide references without implying every recipe is required.

## Conflicts, limitations and version drift

- The only local TMM evidence is the exact packwiz metadata; no mod JAR, client world, server test of mechanics or current registry dump was available in the empty workspace. The upstream source tree's Gradle property matches version 5.13.5, but source-tree equality is not a binary/JAR checksum equivalence.
- The explanatory community wiki used here targets Ars Nouveau 5.11 for NeoForge 1.21.1, not the pinned 5.13.5. It informs the gameplay map only where supported by current upstream code/book strings; exact 5.11 differences may exist.
- Latest 5.13.5 release notes are mostly visual/particle and rendering fixes (including Prestidigitation behavior on living entities wearing/holding affected gear); they do not establish TMM runtime behavior.
- Source link acceptance tags are data driven. A crop, mob, or food being in the pack does not establish a matching Ars tag or supported automation path.
- Drygmy output and Ars summoning are potential loopholes in any drop-gated economy. Audit exact valid mob entities, outputs and Source costs in a disposable world before allowing use on protected boss drops.
- No Ars-Nouveau-only interaction with external fluid, FE, raw XP, storage or item transport networks was verified. Ars addon or third-party recipe integration may add such capabilities, but requires separate evidence.
