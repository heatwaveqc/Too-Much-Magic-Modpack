# Mod Capability Atlas — complete quest-source research framework (draft v0.2)

**Owner correction, 2026-11-20:** The Atlas is the **primary factual research basis for creating the mod's quests**. It is **not a mod ranking**, scoring rubric, single-word checklist, or shortcut to an incomplete 'profile complete'. Each researched axis must disclose actual blocks, specific items, spells, operations, first obtainable version, upgrades, terminal reachable extent, resource inputs, evidence and actionable player steps.

**Owner clarification, 2026-11-23 — Atlas is research, not mod testing or game design.** Treat a pinned, released **third-party mod as functional by default**. Our obligation is to understand and record *what it does and how a player uses it*: source/JAR/registries/data, native guide and progression, inputs/outputs, costs, limits, version scope, and observable effects. **Do not require gameplay testing or write a standing in-game QA checklist just to declare a mod's Atlas research complete.** Source-backed descriptions of normal native functionality are sufficient when the mechanics are unambiguous. The Atlas does **not** redesign, balance, fix, smoke-test or independently certify third-party releases. Quest and integration implementation are separate downstream workstreams.

**Escalation rule (exception, not default):** If the *specific code or data* genuinely leaves a material player-facing action/number/guide step ambiguous, identify the exact unresolved question and first pursue more source, resources, compiled methods or documentation. Recommend a **small targeted observational test only if code-level evidence cannot resolve it**. Likewise, if actual **cross-mod operations** require two mods, combined tags/recipes, datapack priority, config/loader behavior, or Packwiz-specific integration to establish a result, record that as **an integration interaction to check**, preferably with static or automated inspection; only hand off to pack testing when the interacting behavior cannot be determined otherwise. **Do not turn generic possibilities** (UI might differ, every spell might fail, every recipe might not load, normal worldgen might not work) **into mandatory uncertainty** without contrary evidence. Ordinary client acceptance, fun, pacing and questbook usability belong to subsequent **quest design/playtesting**, never the Atlas completion bar. All in-game builds/smokes continue to require existing owner authorization.

This is the **independent, capability-first parallel workstream** tracked by [issue #410](https://github.com/heatwaveqc/Too-Much-Magic-Modpack/issues/410) and [draft PR #411](https://github.com/heatwaveqc/Too-Much-Magic-Modpack/pull/411). Original [Mod Comprehension](../mod-comprehension/WORK-CHAT-INSTRUCTIONS.md) work continues to research progression, inputs/outputs and in-game walkthroughs. The Atlas may cite and reconcile that work without modifying the files of the original project. The two can eventually be integrated by IDs and reviewed links, not by discarding either set of evidence.

**Owner clarification, 2026-11-24 — completion depth:** Cover all significant capabilities and meaningful native unlocks with actionable quest-source steps. Transcribe recipes only where they gate progress, have unusual inputs or require special procedures; ordinary JEI recipes need not be duplicated. Numerical details are required when they materially affect progression, operation, meaningful choices or cross-mod interactions, not every coefficient/timer. Nonblocking reference precision is not a reason to keep a survey open. Preserve substantive findings in the repository on each pass.

**Owner priority correction, 2026-11-24:** Major standalone magic quest chapters take precedence over focused addons, bridges, libraries and encounter-adjacent surveys. Preserve deferred checkpoints; inspect compatibility only when essential to a major native progression gate.

## What one complete *capability* actually looks like

For a named spell, altar operation, item, ritual, summon, production system or player state, the researcher must answer:

- **What exact in-game thing?** Item, block, multiblock, entity, spell, player ability or recipe: localized name, actual ID if verified, and what it does independently of the item carrying it.
- **How do you first unlock and use it?** Guidebook/JEI reference, prerequisites, materials, physical build/recipe, interaction, mode, typed costs and the observable result.
- **What is its bottom end?** Earliest possible operation and functional limits in a new survival world (not 'power 1').
- **How does it progress?** Every meaningful intermediate upgrade and its exact crafting/research/ritual/region/boss/energy gate; whether alternate branches are optional or mutually exclusive.
- **What is its top end?** Its terminal reachable version(s), limits and practical effect, not how it scores relative to another mod.
- **How is it powered?** Name the actual resource and the true block, player, item or world container; track generation, transfer, spending and failure/overflow. Never silently translate unlike pools into FE.
- **How would a quest teach it?** Concrete ordered actions, guide/JEI reference, visible result, truthful FTB detection predicate versus inventory proxy, proposed positive XP and safe thematic item reward, and an exact positive/boundary acceptance test.
- **How certain is the claim?** Immutable pinned-JAR/source evidence, current pack configuration, proven native/installed compatibility and whether any client/world tests have actually been performed.

A *staff* with a damaging spell creates an equipment record under Gear and a distinct functional record under Direct Damage. A mana→FE converter gets resource-flow records under Energy and a verified compatibility bridge. A Neo Vitae ritual may simultaneously require altar tier, EV storage, particular blocks and a spell effect: keep distinct records linked by stable capability IDs, not five duplicated unconnected descriptions.

## How to research a mod

1. **Identity and onboarding.** Confirm the current selected mod ID, exact pack pin/hash (not just the desired manifest), compatible addons, official guidebook, JEI categories and how the first player reaches a functioning setup. Recover relevant material from the existing deep Mod Comprehension profile with source links; don't assume older descriptions prove the currently pinned JAR.
2. **Actual objects.** Make inventories for all major named blocks/multiblocks, items/gear, rituals/spells/abilities, mobs and world features. Preserve registry IDs and recipe/source references. Exhaustive bulk lists may live in a linked secondary table, but each distinct gameplay system needs a fully explained entry.
3. **Capabilities.** Investigate every supported axis, create detailed records per independent action/effect and multi-tag where appropriate. Map earliest use → intermediate developments → maximal reachable output(s) with objects, exact mechanics and risks, rather than leaving one-word 'present' summaries.
4. **Resource systems.** Audit every currency/power/channel by producer, storage in **block**, **player**, **item** and other locations, capacity, persistence, transfer, consumers and progression. Neo Vitae's block EV, item Orb EV and personal Anima paths are the grounding example, **not an invitation to treat their units as interchangeable**.
5. **Progression graph.** List *all* formal tiers/stages, exact unlock actions, new block/item/spell/energy options, optional branches, and endgame. Neo Vitae's Ara Vitae 0–5 and Astral Sorcery's research/altar/attunement progression require separate detailed transition nodes.
6. **Quest handoff (research, not authoring or testing).** Describe a complete native progression spine and meaningful optional branches as possible quest-source material. Each proposed teaching entry should identify actual player steps, native guide/JEI reference, visible success, supported/provisional detection predicate, prerequisites and safe reward considerations. Source-backed behavior is the acceptance basis for the Atlas. **Do not require positive/boundary gameplay tests** for each native mechanic or treat these research entries as implemented/accepted FTB tasks; any later quest behavior testing belongs to the quest/integration owner.
7. **Research review.** A `source-backed-partial` profile is useful and honest; mark `quest-source-ready` when all required native progression tiers, core resource paths and significant capabilities are sufficiently explained for a player-facing walkthrough without unacknowledged material *source* gaps. **No in-game test, client acceptance or pack-wide integration test is required for that research status.** If a *particular* source ambiguity or cross-mod edge cannot be resolved from code/data, state it precisely; it does not invalidate unrelated completed research.

## Evidence and status semantics

Each axis coverage row is `unknown`, `present`, `no_capability_confirmed` (cite a thorough scoped search) or `not_applicable` (explain). A capability's presence is separately `native_upstream`, `present_in_pack_unverified`, `verified_in_pack`, `pack_customization_proposed`, or `unsupported_unknown`. Evidence grades remain `[pin]`, `[jar: SHA]`, `[source: SHA]`, `[docs: version]`, `[pack-test: build/world]`, `[inference]`. **Source-verified or pinned-binary-verified is a successful research result, not a failed/unfinished gameplay test.** Only use `verified_in_pack` and `[pack-test]` when that *separate* integration evidence actually exists. Neither status is mandatory for Atlas completion. Never invent runtime PASS from source inspection, and do not invent a need for runtime testing merely because the evidence is static.

## Ownership and change boundary

Atlas work owns only `docs/mod-capabilities/`; do not modify mod selection/manifest, scripts, gameplay data, quest files or existing `docs/mod-comprehension/` in this workstream. Quest designs/rewards are source-based suggestions for downstream owners, not implementations or requests for playtesting. **No Minecraft builds, integration assembly, server boots, gameplay tests, or Prism packages are routine Atlas deliverables**; any exceptional test must answer a concrete unresolved code or cross-mod question and obey existing authorization. Work in the current coordinated research branch/PR or use a single isolated reviewable PR and merge promptly, rather than creating a fresh lingering PR per research pass.
